# Drift Lab W — plain keyboard steering, reference-parity cars

Branch `lead/drift-lab-w` (from `lead/drift-lab-c`, with `lead/round12` merged for
`docs/reference/wreckfest-keyboard.md`). Owner verdict on Drift Lab C: "nothing like" the
reference — steering response, the artificial-feeling drift hold, throttle/power. Drift Lab W
is deliberately **plain**: no intent modes, nothing smart. The point is to find out what the
car itself does when the steering is as simple as the reference's.

## Headline (step 2 measurements)

1. **With plain steering, the owner's core technique does not work in our physics.** Countersteer
   key held + full throttle in 2nd spins every RWD car (the four parity cars, shiro_turbo and
   rampart_50) within ~0.5–1.3 s. This happens with the reference rack timing, with a 0.15 s rack,
   and **even with a near-instant rack (0.02 s)**, and at 30–70 % throttle as well as full. So
   the rack speed is not what stops a held drift. Once a slide starts, our cars run away from a
   full countersteer. In the reference (clip 1) the same input holds a steady 30–45° drift for
   4+ s. Drift Labs B / C hid this with an assist that computed the countersteer.
2. **Steering response matches the reference mechanically.** A 0.25 s tap at 60 km/h is most of
   a full-lock flick (≈28° of 38° in the muscle cars), as clip 7 describes. At 140 km/h the speed
   sensitivity cuts it to ≈16°. The tap tables are in the plausible range for clip 2's racing
   taps (0.1 s ≈ 5–6° of heading).
3. **Handbrake pivots are slow while the lever is held**, and the catch then fails (as in 1).
   While held: ≈6° of rotation in 0.3 s, 25° in 0.6 s, 62° in 1.0 s (RWD-B: 10 / 39 / 93°).
   The reference turns ~150–180° in a 1 s hairpin. Afterwards counter + throttle spins the RWD
   cars. The FWD car (parity_fwd_c) pivots and is pulled straight by the throttle in 0.3–0.6 s,
   which matches clip 3's recovery.
4. **Next gap is the car, not the steering.** Candidates, in the order I'd check them: rear
   lateral grip under wheelspin (combined slip: how much side force a spinning rear keeps), the
   cars' max steering lock (≈38°; clip 1 holds 30–45° of slide, so full counter lock barely
   covers the slide angle), yaw inertia / damping, and the rear LSD lock. The pure lateral curve
   is not it: `sport` keeps 88 % of peak at 30° and 86 % at 40°. Owner's picks map onto this:
   "drift hold feels artificial" (B / C's assist) → plain has none, but the car can't hold;
   "throttle / power" → the parity cars give the reference's W/kg, and the runaway happens at
   every throttle.

**How to try it:** `?drift=w` (default car `parity_rwd_d`, the clip-1 drift car), or
`?drift=w&car=parity_rwd_a`, or the start menu's *Drift Lab W*. The *tune* panel has the car
selector and the steering constants.

## Step 1 — raw keys, plain constant-rate rack, reference cars

- **Raw keyboard** (`apps/testbed-web/src/input.ts`, `setRawKeyboard`, on only in the lab):
  steer −1 / 0 / +1 and throttle 0 / 1 reach the sim with no ramp.
- **Plain steering** (`crates/wp_vehicle/src/plain.rs`, process-wide lab override like
  `drift::set_tuning_override`): the rack moves at a constant rate toward `key × lock(speed)`:
  full lock in `t_full` (0.28 s), back to centre in `t_return` (0.33 s); a reversal runs at the
  return rate to centre, then at the full-lock rate out. Lock vs speed:
  `lock_frac = 1 / (1 + s · (v / v_ref)^p)`, s = speed sensitivity (75 %), v_ref = 113 km/h,
  p = 2 → 100 % at walking pace, 83 % at 60, 63 % at 100, 47 % at 140, 40 % at 160 km/h.
  Optional *self-align* (default 0): released keys return toward `self_align × front axle's
  travel` instead of the centre (A/B only; no reference data for released keys mid-drift).
- With plain on, `Vehicle::step` skips the drift intent modes (hold / angle-up / transition /
  catch / envelope), the slide-throttle feathering, the drift throttle shaping and the pedal
  filter: the engine gets the throttle key. Physics is the same in Arcade and Simulation.
  Steering-model state is not stored (the rack is recovered from the centre angle), so
  snapshots / rewinds need nothing new.
- **Assists in the lab** (owner's reference): TCS off, ESC off, ABS on, manual gears with the
  automatic clutch, no pedal filters. TCS Off/Half/Full: `TcsLevel` has not landed on
  `lead/round12` yet, so TCS stays off. No `lead/wf-parity` branch existed, so the four cars
  were built here.
- **Reference-parity test cars** (`data/cars/parity/`, generated by
  `tools/parity/build_parity_cars.py`): power, torque, mass, gear count and drive from the
  reference; geometry, tyres (`sport`), suspension, brakes, lattice from the nearest roster car
  scaled with mass (springs / dampers / ARBs / brakes × mass ratio). Not in the roster: the
  roster scans are non-recursive, and wasm embeds them under their own kind (`parity_car_ids()`,
  not `car_ids()`), loadable by id.

## Step 2 — measurements

`cargo run --release -p wp_vehicle --example drift_lab_w` (all tables), `… -- plain`,
`… -- sweep [cars]` (constants A/B + throttle diagnostic), `… -- trace parity_rwd_d
[naive|good|into]` (tick trace). `PLAIN='{"t_full_s":0.2}'` to try constants, `PLAIN=off` for
the normal steering model with the same assists. All runs: TCS off, ESC off, ABS on, manual
gears (2nd for drifts), countersteer help 0. Drift runs: 40 m circle at 0.6 g, enter with the key
into the turn + throttle, then 6 s. "drift s" = time at 10–50° of body slip.

5. Cars: parity_rwd_a / b / d / fwd_c vs shiro_turbo / rampart_50 (selector in the panel). Does
   the extra torque per kg feel like the reference's power?

## Step 3 — suspension knobs (owner: "far too switchy") and steering at speed (clip 8)

**Steering at speed (clip 8):** the plain rack already works that way: `t_full` / `t_return`
are the same at every speed, and the speed sensitivity scales only the lock (amount), never the
time. Covered by `tests/drift_lab_w.rs` (full lock time at walking pace, lock fraction at
160 km/h).

**Ride tuning override** (`crates/wp_vehicle/src/ride_lab.rs`, lab only, process-wide like
the plain steering, unchanged/bit-identical when off): scales for springs, anti-roll bars,
damper bump, damper rebound (all corners), and the **roll-centre / pitch-centre height**:
where the tyres' lateral / longitudinal force acts on the body (0 = contact patch = the data).
Panel section "suspension" with *lab default* / *car data* buttons; the readout shows the
body roll now and the roll / pitch model (Hz, ζ, °/g) of the current car. wasm:
`ride_tune_json()`, `ride_tune_defaults_json()`, `ride_tune_set(json)`, `ride_readout(entity)`.
Caveat on the roll centre: the link-borne load transfer is not added back to the tyres, so a
high roll centre also lowers total lateral load transfer a little.

**Lab default** (`RideTune::lab_default()`): springs × 0.9, bars × 0.25, dampers × 1.4 bump /
× 1.8 rebound, roll / pitch centre 0. Why not softer: at springs × 0.65 the front sits 1–12 mm
from the bump stop at rest and the body rides the stops (a stiff 2.3–2.5 Hz mode shows up in the
roll kick). Going below ~1.4 Hz needs softer springs **with ride height / preload compensated**,
which is a car-data change. Bars and dampers alone can't get below ~1.4–1.5 Hz because the
springs carry most of the roll stiffness.

**Before / after** (`cargo run --release -p wp_vehicle --example drift_lab_w -- ride`):
*model* = linear roll model (springs + bars at the wheels, roll about the ground, rigid tyres);
*kick* = fitted free response of the real model to a 0.15 rad/s roll kick at rest (0 / 1.00 =
no overshoot: at or past critical); lane change = keyboard left 0.3 s / right 0.45 s / left
0.15 s at 100 km/h (≈60 mph); S-bend = left 0.6 / release 0.2 / right 1.0 / release 0.2 /
left 0.6 s at 70 km/h. *rocks* = roll-rate reversals after the keys are released.

## Step 4 — car / tyre experiments: what would hold "counter key + full throttle"

Lab-only override `crates/wp_vehicle/src/car_lab.rs` (`CarTune`; process-wide like the others,
bit-identical when off: the C test values are unchanged to the last digit). Panel section "car /
tyre experiments" (starts at the data; *drift candidate* / *tyre data* buttons). wasm:
`car_tune_json()`, `car_tune_defaults_json()`, `car_tune_set(json)`. Knobs:

- **side grip under spin** (`lat_under_spin`, 1 = data): how much a tyre's longitudinal slip
  counts against its *lateral* force in the combined-slip model (rear only by default). Drive
  force unchanged (`tire::combined_forces_weighted`; = `combined_forces` at 1).
- **rear / front peak slip ×**: the lateral curve stretched along the slip axis (peak slip
  angle × k, Magic Formula B ÷ k).
- **steering lock ×**, **rear diff locked**, **engine torque ×**.
- **yaw damping (diag)**: *not physical*, a yaw torque −k·m·L²·r. It measures how much damping
  a held drift needs.
- Offline only (example env `YAW_INERTIA=1.3`): yaw inertia × k.

Why it spins (trace, `… --example drift_lab_w -- ptrace`, parity_rwd_d, counter key at 6° + full
throttle, data): the rear wheels spin at once (slip ratio ≈ 1 by 0.35 s, up to 10 later; 807 N·m
in 2nd asks ≈ 12 kN of rear drive vs ≈ 10 kN of rear grip). In the combined-slip model the rear
force then points mostly forward, so rear **side** force falls to 1.5–3 kN. Meanwhile the fronts,
even at full counter lock, never get past the front axle's travel (the travel runs away
faster), so their ≈ 6 kN keeps pushing the nose *into* the turn. With rear side grip kept
(`lat_under_spin` 0.25 + lock 1.3) the held key **does** catch the slide, then over-corrects:
the car swings through and spins the other way. There is no damping from the saturated rear.

**Single knobs** (parity_rwd_d, plain steering, lab ride default; drift = 6 s on the 40 m

Rear peak slip × 2–3 on top makes it worse (grip driving gets loose). Yaw inertia doesn't help:
it slows the slide down but doesn't damp it. Test: `tests/drift_lab_w_car.rs` (the data spins,
the candidate holds > 3.5 s at 15–40°).

**Conclusions for A (tyre / car data owner):**
1. Two things are missing at once: **rear side force under wheelspin** (our friction ellipse
   gives a spinning rear almost no side grip) and **yaw damping in a slide** (once the rear is
   saturated nothing resists yaw rate). Fix one without the other and the held key either
   still spins (side grip only) or the car can't slide at all (side grip 0).
2. The physical source of the damping is still open. Our rear force is flat past ≈ 6° of slip
   in a slide, so it can't damp. Something has to make it grow with slip angle or yaw rate. A
   later / wider peak does that but ruins grip driving here. Candidates to try in the tyre
   model: a combined-slip shape where side force keeps rising with slip angle at high slip ratio
   (not just a smaller longitudinal weight), and a tyre lag / relaxation that doesn't
   destabilise.
3. Steering lock, diff locking, torque and yaw inertia are not the lever (none helps alone;
   lock × 0.9 is a small refinement with the candidate).
4. The candidate is still **not steady** (slide angle sd ≈ 10° vs clip 1's "steady for several
   seconds"), and it trims tap response by ~25 %.
